A smaller, clearer drawing workspace

Apply shared sizing and icon rules first. Then reorganize the drawing workflow in focused passes, preserving the controls and pen behavior that already work.

This addendum enhances the supplied expert review. The plan was drafted against 0.50.1. Pass 1 shipped shared drawing metrics, icons, tabs and property rows in 0.51.0. What to retain, and what to refine

Keep the review’s direction

Put brush selection, size presets and quick properties together. Keep Layers dependable, retain native docking, scroll content independently and pin completion actions. Retain visual brush previews, exact entry and canvas handles.

Correct its density baseline

Production already has a 44 px tool rail, 32 px named tool rows and 28 px ordinary action targets. The review’s 48 px rail, 36–40 px rows and 32 px ordinary targets would enlarge those controls.

Give icons consistent meanings

There are already 92 bundled Lucide SVGs. The problem includes reuse: Arc and Bézier share a symbol; Texture, Tones, Guides and Warp share another. A feature can also change symbol between Tools and Properties.

Treat coverage as an inventory

The CSVs contain 451 unique screen IDs across 39 families and 1,576 control entries across 37 families. Only 98 screen rows are marked Workspace concept; 353 still need detailed layouts. Mapping a control does not verify its redesigned behavior.

Review method for this addendum: read the supplied review and UI guidelines, inspected both supplied workspace images, checked CSV counts, and audited shared controls, theme and icon mappings in source. This is not a new full-resolution or native-interaction audit of all 451 screens.

Proposed common rules

  1. Reduce excess structure first. Use smaller ordinary glyphs, consistent padding, fewer repeated headings and tighter rows. Preserve usable click/pen areas. Brush samples, reference images, color pickers and curve editors keep the space needed to communicate their content.
  2. One feature has one recognizable identity. Reuse its icon and name across Tools, Properties, menus and Quick access. Different operations need distinguishable silhouettes. Common verbs such as Add, Delete and Duplicate should remain consistent everywhere.
  3. Keep the everyday loop direct. Selecting a visible brush, applying a size preset, choosing a swatch and selecting a layer each remain one activation. Organization belongs in management surfaces; detailed brush configuration stays behind the wrench.
  4. Use text to make unfamiliar things learnable. Keep tool names available, visible property labels, layer names, blend modes, exact values and meaningful On/Off states. Use icons for familiar actions. Do not require beginners to learn a panel of unexplained symbols.
  5. Use one property-row pattern. Label, slider and compact exact field share a row when they fit. Long labels wrap above the editor. Keep the 48 px numeric field, omit unit suffixes inside it, retain full precision and expose units in the label/help. It is not a three-digit value limit.
  6. Show relevant controls in stable places. Hide complete irrelevant rows and measure the active page. Keep currently relevant but temporarily blocked actions in place, with a reason. Do not reshuffle controls during pen contact or while values are being typed.
  7. Keep selection, focus and errors visibly different. Use restrained gray selection plus an edge/check indicator, a clear keyboard-focus outline and a stable status line with a reason and recovery action where appropriate. Color alone must not carry meaning.
  8. Make discovery available without permanent instructions. Keep the shared 600 ms tooltip delay, action names and shortcuts. Empty states get one concise next action. Preserve labels in tool-list mode and use short labels for unfamiliar categories.
  9. Protect the pen workflow. Returning to the canvas restores its correct cursor immediately. Tool activation returns drawing focus; editing a field retains its focus. No hover-triggered drawers, layout switches, thumbnail rebuilding or library writes during pen contact.
  10. Preserve the artist’s setup. Keep tools, custom icons, tags, colors, groups, shortcuts, saved panel geometry, numeric bounds, Undo and pending-edit decisions. New workspace arrangements apply through an explicit preset or reset, rather than replacing a saved layout.

One coherent drawing flow

Left: choose and adjust
Toolbox → tool list → size presets → quick properties. Keep these adjacent as a default dock stack while preserving their independent dock identities. The list must continue to support Shapes, Guides and other groups; do not rename the generic Tools panel permanently to Brushes.
Center: draw
Canvas gets remaining space. Quick access stays available in drawing mode. Selection/transform completion actions stay visible outside scrolling settings and away from pen contact. Production-sheet margins and metadata retain their existing meaning.
Right: color, references and layers
Trial Colors / References / Navigator tabs above Layers. Layers remains the default lower surface; History and Auto Actions remain reachable optional tabs/docks. Artists can keep Colors and References side by side by undocking them.
Small windows: deliberate adaptation · implemented in 0.57.0
Compact drawing opens the existing Tools / Sizes / Properties as tabs in an overlay, using the persistent current-tool/size button beside the foreground swatch. Opening and closing preserves the canvas viewport, zoom and pan. Close or Escape returns to drawing; editors keep their own Escape behavior. The drawer fits the available canvas and retains its page/open state. The normal Drawing preset restores the original docks. Numeric drafts, gestures and popups block unsafe changes. Validate the explicit workflow on the physical Huion before considering automatic adaptation; no breakpoint or hover activation is implemented.
Advanced editing: separate, consistent surfaces
Wrench opens advanced brush settings; a library action opens brush management. Browsing the library must not change the current brush. Keep Add/Import/Use visible, show selected-brush metadata together, and expose occasional bulk actions through a contextual menu.

Beginner experience: one predictable default workspace, readable tool names, distinct icons, concise empty states and reversible actions. Avoid adding a separate “beginner app” with missing features. Offer labels and comfortable sizing without changing the workflow.

Theme direction

Refine the existing neutral Spectrum-inspired theme. Proposed review colors are good candidates, subject to native checks: canvas #191919; dock #252525; raised #303030; text #ededed; secondary #b7b7b7; focus #dedede.

A #484848 selection on #252525 has only about 1.68:1 contrast. Pair it with a persistent high-contrast edge/check; do not rely on the fill alone. Secondary text #b7b7b7 on that selected fill is about 4.56:1, so avoid further dimming or opacity blending there. These are calculated opaque-color ratios, not a complete accessibility audit. Theme changes affect editor chrome only.

How to decide whether it is better

0.56.0 layout evidence includes 16 native and 16 offscreen 2× views, with representative narrow and enlarged-text review. Drawing fits 1440 × 900. The requested 1024 × 768 full-layout trial expands to 1067 pixels wide natively or 1087 offscreen; Compact drawing fits 900 × 768 with controls closed. In that 0.56.0 build, opening all drawing controls still needed the wider dock layout, and the overlay drawer was still a proposal. Comparable canvas area was unchanged from 0.55.0. The 0.57.0 follow-up replaces that compact open stack with the overlay described above. These captures establish layout; active keyboard focus is verified separately by the later unlocked native suite run.

  • Space: measure canvas area, visible tool/layer rows and scrolling at the same font scale, window geometry and open panels. Trial 1440 × 900 and 1024 × 768 logical windows, plus a 900 px compact stress case. Do not promise an arbitrary percentage before measurement.
  • Learning: observe a new user choosing a brush, changing size/color, creating a layer, undoing a mistake and opening advanced settings. Record completion, misclicks, requests for help and unnecessary steps.
  • Pen: cross dock separators into the canvas, switch brushes, use sliders/presets and resume a fast stroke. Validate with the Huion; synthetic tests cannot establish physical comfort.
  • State: check empty/filter-no-results, disabled-with-reason, loading/error, long names, locked layers, unsaved numeric drafts, pending stamp/transform, large libraries and narrow floating docks.
  • Access: visible focus, logical keyboard order, accessible names/states and the 600 ms tooltip rule. Larger fonts must reflow. Use 4.5:1 normal-text and 3:1 essential-control/state contrast as design targets, checked against actual adjacent colors.
  • Performance and preservation: no additional panel rebuilds or thumbnail generation during pen contact. Compare stroke/redraw timings. Verify dock restoration, custom icons/groups, exact entry and existing Undo/Apply/Discard/Cancel behavior.
